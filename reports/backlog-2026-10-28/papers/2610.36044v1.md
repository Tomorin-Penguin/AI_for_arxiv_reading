## 作者英文摘要要点转述 / English abstract paraphrase

The authors construct a Schwinger–Keldysh influence functional for a matter-wave interferometer coupled to a dark-matter environment, then project the field-theoretic response to the worldlines of its arms. Retarded environmental kernels enter interference phase shifts, while Keldysh fluctuations suppress coherence. The formalism treats independent atom clouds and rigid composite probes separately and studies contact scattering as well as light-mediator interactions, including possible tachyonic in-medium mediator masses. Prospective sensitivities are conditional on benchmark apparatus and dark-matter parameters.

**English:** The measurable quantity is an interferometer reduced-density-matrix element rather than an energy-deposition rate. An open real-time effective action distinguishes causal response (`K_r`, phase) from environment fluctuations (`K_k`, contrast loss). In the controlled heavy-probe limit, real and imaginary influence phases directly determine the interference fringes. Composite rigid probes can exhibit coherent `N_at²` scaling in both channels, whereas the independent-atom system shows a cloud-enhanced phase but no analogous leading `N_at` enhancement for the individual-atom decoherence observable. Long-range scalar mediators require additional medium-stability assumptions. These are model- and protocol-dependent measurement statements, not universal gravitational-memory predictions.
